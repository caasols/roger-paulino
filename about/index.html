About

Roger Paulino was born in Pretoria, South Africa, in 1986, and lives and works in Leipzig, Germany. As the child of immigrants growing up in the fractured society of apartheid, he was introduced to drawing at an early age by watching the Khoi-San people paint African landscapes onto animal hides from the savanna. Due to the prevailing violence, his family relocated to the island of Madeira, where he experienced a simple island lifestyle and traditional farming methods, and came into contact with the skate subculture that served as his initial gateway to art.

His work draws from diverse sources and manifests in a wide variety of forms, exploring life's mysteries and the small, chance occurrences that shape our perception of reality. For the last decade he has dedicated himself to mastering the techniques of printing, one of the reasons he moved to Germany, the land of the Gutenberg press and the Northern Renaissance. He does not call his prints prints, but rather paintings; they live in a limbo between the two, printed once, layer over layer, until the end is reached.
